ENEMY TRAITS BUILDER

Things enemies can do that make a world harder: wind-up, grow, overload and ricochet. This makes one file, com.sugarheady.gamemodeforge.enemytraits.rules.json, for your BepInEx\config folder (beside the .cfg files). Enemy Traits writes the defaults there the first time it runs. No restart needed: the rules are read when a world begins — at each New Game Plus jump, and at the start of a run — and a world keeps the traits it began with, through every save and Continue.

Presets

The shipped rules: all four traits on every enemy, the Queens included, from NEW GAME PLUS 1. The first world is the game as it comes.
One more trait every world: wind-up from NEW GAME PLUS 1, grow from 2, ricochet from 3, overload from 4. Everything else as DEFAULT.
For trying it out: all four from the first world (so no Queens to kill first, like New Game Plus's own TEST), on every enemy, strong and quick so you can see them: wind-up full in 1 s at x2 fire rate, overload full after 2 s of firing at x3 for 4 s, shots swelling to x3, 3 bounces that turn toward you. Switch the overload gauge on in the GAME MODE FORGE tab to watch the meters.
All four from the first world (plus 0) — harder enemies without New Game Plus, where every world is plus 0. With New Game Plus too, they grow each plus from there.
No traits at all.

Every trait

A volley is a scripted pattern that fires on its own timer — the Queen's bullet patterns, the crosses, the Fly Zapper. While a pattern runs, the enemy counts as holding the trigger (gaps included) for wind-up and overload. With speed (the default) a wound-up or overloading enemy's patterns come faster too; count leaves their pace alone; off leaves volleys out of the meters.
Off by default: an enemy can have every trait it rolls. With it on, an enemy that rolled more keeps that many, picked by its own seed (the same ones after a Continue).

How the numbers stack

Every number has three boxes: its value, what it gains a plus, and a cap (blank = none). It counts from its trait's own from plus: value + gain × (plus − from plus), stopped at the cap — so wind-up's x1.5 fire rate, +0.25 a plus, cap 3 is x1.5 in NEW GAME PLUS 1, x1.75 in 2, x2 in 3. A number that shrinks (a negative gain) stops at its cap from above. Coverage is the share of enemies a trait reaches; which ones is fixed per enemy. Without New Game Plus every world is plus 0.

In the game instead

    
    
      
  • The GAME MODE FORGE tab in Options has Enemy Traits' own switch and, under it,
      one row per trait (Trait: wind-up, grow, overload, ricochet), the
      Volleys slider and the limit — each writes this same file, keeping
      everything else, and applies from the next world. A trait's row says when it needs New Game
      Plus.
    • 
      
  • Marker on enemies with traits (off by default) puts New Game Plus's amber ring beside
      an enemy that got a trait not every enemy gets. Overload gauge on enemies (off by
      default) draws a very small bar over every enemy with overload: blue as it fills, gold while it
      is overloaded. Nothing else on screen tells you an enemy is wound up or overloading. Both are
      in com.sugarheady.gamemodeforge.enemytraits.cfg and apply at once.
    • 
      
  • With New Game Plus installed, its WORLD INFO lists this world's traits with their
      numbers and how many enemies have each, and its victory screen names the next world's.

What each trait does

    
    
      
  • Wind-up: the longer an enemy fires, the better its guns get — full after the
      wind-up time, back down when it stops. Its rewards are Module Forge's windup rewards.
    • 
      
  • Overload: a hidden meter fills as the enemy fires (or with its shots, or its hits on
      you); when it is full the enemy overloads and its guns get better for the window. No overheat,
      no lockout — only the good half.
    • 
      
  • Grow: enemy shots swell as they fly, hitbox included. Size only: damage is New Game
      Plus's enemy damage.
    • 
      
  • Ricochet: enemy shots bounce off walls. Only terrain is a bounce — touching you,
      your drones, minions or wingmen ends the shot. A rocket that bounces loses its lock on you and
      flies off the wall (with toward you on it locks back on). The two bullets that already
      bounce, and the Queen's and the bombers' drifting mines, keep their own behaviour.
    • 
      
  • Fire rate, from either meter, also makes an enemy beam hit you more often (a
      beam's damage comes in steps, not shots) and an ammo gun cheaper a shot (or a faster gun would
      only empty sooner).
    • 
      
  • Every enemy that gets a trait keeps it; your own drones and minions never get one. A bullet
      you knock back keeps its grow and its bounces.
